# Prompt 04 — the music (Suno v5.5, style prompt)

Same trick as the video: I started from a prompt for **1970s outlaw country from Alabama** and mapped it line for line onto the sound that was actually coming out of Nairobi estate radios in the 80s — Benga. Not "swap in African instruments." Each line has a job (lead instrument, texture, rhythm feel, production era, energy), and each job gets the Benga equivalent.

```
1980s Kenyan Benga, ultra authentic retro East African guitar-pop pure instrumental, lively Nairobi estate courtyard vibe, interlocking electric guitar lines trading call-and-response melodies, rolling melodic electric bassline driving the groove, tight hi-hat-led drum kit shuffle, bright chiming rhythm guitar, occasional soukous-style guitar flourishes, warm analog 80s cassette-tape saturated sound with cheap boombox warmth and tape hiss, joyful communal party energy mixed with nostalgic scratchy charm, 128 BPM, C major, 4/4 driving groove, pure instrumental no vocals no singing
```

The mapping, for anyone adapting this to another place and time:

| Country original | Benga version | The job |
|---|---|---|
| heavy banjo picking, fiddle leads | interlocking electric guitars, call-and-response | lead voices |
| weeping pedal steel, slide guitar | soukous-style flourishes | the "extra flavor" layer |
| solid upright bass | rolling *melodic* electric bass | Benga bass is practically a lead instrument |
| brush drums, lazy shuffle | hi-hat-led kit, faster | tempo feel (115 → 128 BPM) |
| 70s tape + vinyl warmth | 80s cassette + boombox + hiss | period-correct media. Kenya in the 80s was cassette, not vinyl |

Suno delivered 129.2 BPM against 128 asked. Track is 2:30, which matters: the alignment script needs room to slide a 30–55 s window around and find where the beats land on the cuts.

For a different setting, keep the *structure* of the prompt and swap the genre facts. Swahili Rumba (Les Wanyika era) works for a more urban/coastal Nairobi feel; Taarab or Chakacha for Mombasa.
